# Density as Translation Field Between Coherent Regimes

## What is this?
A foundational physics artifact in the Structural Flow project.

This paper asks what changes once G is no longer treated as “just a constant,” but as a structurally load-bearing translation-strength term inside a deeper Density layer.

## Why should I care?
If the argument works, this is not only a paper about one constant.

It becomes a paper about whether Density can lawfully connect structural description and physical expression without collapsing the difference between them.

That matters for how we think about G, c, ħ, the Planck scale, and what still remains genuinely empirical.

## What is the claim?
The paper argues that:
- G is best understood as translation strength
- G, c, and ħ are best understood as measurable faces of one Density translation layer
- the Planck scale is structurally load-bearing as the simultaneous face-minimum of Boundary
- the remaining empirical burden may reduce to one anchor rather than many

The larger capstone claim is that Density can function as a translation field between coherent regimes.

## What is this not?
This artifact is not:
- a replacement for general relativity
- a derivation of every part of physics
- a claim that the numerical value of G was deduced from the primitive alone
- a loose metaphor about “everything being connected”

It is a derivation-first foundational artifact with a strict boundary between structural forcing and empirical anchoring.

## How was this done?
The paper works downstream from the existing Structural Flow canon and prior density / black-hole results.

It proceeds in proof order:
1. G as translation strength
2. the constant layer of G, c, and ħ
3. the Planck floor
4. why c appears cubed
5. one empirical anchor
6. the capstone claim about Density as translation field
